# BloodRadar — Collaborator Testing Guide

Thank you for taking the time to look at this. This guide is meant to help you decide honestly whether BloodRadar is worth partnering with, piloting, or contributing to — not to convince you it already is.

## What this is
BloodRadar helps people in Islamabad & Rawalpindi find compatible blood donors faster during emergencies. It's built by a solo student founder, currently in early testing — not yet a funded company or a finished product.

## What this is NOT (please read first)
- It is **not** connected to any real hospital's live blood inventory. Any stock numbers you see are placeholder/demo data.
- It does **not** yet have a large real donor base — the matching you'll see during testing reflects a small test dataset, not real-world reach.
- It has **not** been used in a real emergency yet.

We're sharing it at this early stage on purpose — to get honest feedback before investing further, not to present it as a finished solution.

## What to actually test (15–20 minutes)

1. **Submit a test SOS request** (use a fake name, your real phone number if you're comfortable, or a test number). Notice: how many fields are required, how fast you get a response, and what the confirmation tells you.
2. **Try the "Share on WhatsApp" button** on the confirmation screen — see what message it generates.
3. **Register as a test donor.** Notice: you're listed immediately, no waiting. Then try the phone verification step — it's optional, see how it feels.
4. **Log a test donation** from your donor profile and see the badge system respond.
5. **Open the chatbot** and ask it a few real questions a patient's family member might ask.
6. **Check the "Real ML Lab" tab** — it will likely tell you honestly that there isn't enough real data yet to make predictions. That's intentional, not a bug.

## The questions we actually want your honest answers to

- Does this solve a real problem you've personally seen or dealt with?
- If you work with a hospital, blood bank, or NGO: what would you need to see before trusting this with real patient data or real donor coordination?
- Is there anything here that feels unsafe, misleading, or not ready for real use?
- What's missing that would change your answer from "maybe" to "yes, let's pilot this"?
- What's missing that would make you say no?

Please be direct — a clear "this isn't ready because X" is more useful than polite encouragement.
